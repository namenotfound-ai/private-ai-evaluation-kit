A buyer's guide · Private AI

Before you rent the intelligence, measure what you need to own.

A practical, vendor-neutral checklist for deciding whether an AI workload should stay hosted, move private, or be built around infrastructure you control.

Read the full kit ↗ Talk through a workload
The model is one part of the system. Data boundaries, runtime, memory, tools, evaluation, operations, and exit paths decide whether the system remains useful when requirements change.
01 — Evaluate the whole system

Seven questions that expose the real tradeoffs.

01

What is the workload?

Define the real inputs, context horizon, actions, risk, latency, volume, and human review—not a demo prompt.

02

What does “private” mean?

Separate endpoint privacy from control of weights, memory, runtime, tools, logs, and adaptations.

03

Can you reproduce a result?

Pin versions and capture inputs, evidence, configuration, tool calls, approvals, outputs, and costs.

04

What is the cost per success?

Measure compute, storage, retries, operators, incidents, and the cost of reviewed successful tasks.

05

Who owns the adaptations?

Clarify rights to prompts, feedback, memory, fine-tunes, workflow logic, and the data that made the system useful.

06

What happens when it fails?

Test abstention, escalation, tool errors, interruptions, recovery, upgrades, rollback, and prompt injection.

07

What is the exit path?

Export data, evaluation sets, configurations, and state. Verify you can change models without rewriting everything.

08

Can the vendor prove it?

Run the same representative workload on your data with explicit acceptance criteria and a holdout set.

Do not let a lower per-token price hide a higher cost per successful, reviewed task.

02 — Choose deliberately

Hosted, private, or owned infrastructure can all be right.

Hosted access

Choose it when speed and convenience dominate. Confirm retention, training use, regions, limits, model-change notice, export, and total cost.

Private deployment

Choose it when data boundaries and operational control matter, but you want implementation support and a defined upgrade path.

Build around your stack

Choose it when the model and surrounding system are core capability. Own the artifacts, runtime, evaluation harness, and skills to operate it.

03 — Put it to work

Bring one real workload. Leave with a clearer decision.
